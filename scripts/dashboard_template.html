Findings

Four things the data insists on

Everything here comes out of the same 3,600 cleaned listings the model is trained on.

Two markets wearing one city's name

400 sampled listings · price (₹ crore) against built-up area (sq.ft.)

Composition

Flats and houses barely share a price scale

The cleaned dataset holds 2,799 flats and 801 houses. Median flat: ₹1.39 Cr. Median house: ₹4.25 Cr — about 3.1× more. That gap is why is_house earns 8.5% of the model's importance despite being a single 0/1 flag.

Location

Location is the second lever, not the first

Median prices across the 45 well-covered sectors run from ₹0.41 Cr to ₹12.5 Cr — a 30× spread end to end. Yet the sector's ₹/sq.ft. carries 19.5% of the model's importance against area's 53.7%. Where you buy sets the rate; how much you buy sets the bill.

Simpson's paradox

Old stock looks priciest until you split it

Rank ₹/sq.ft. by age across all listings and Old Property tops the table. Split by type and it inverts: 58% of old listings are houses, and houses sit at ₹20,000/sq.ft. against ₹8,367 for flats.

Within flats alone, the oldest stock is ₹8,886/sq.ft. — below under-construction at ₹9,502. The age effect in the aggregate was property type in disguise.

Baselines

The rule of thumb gets two-thirds of the way

Pricing by sector-median ₹/sq.ft. × area explains 65.3% of the variance on its own. A flat city-wide ₹/sq.ft. explains only 29.9%. The model reaches 80.6% — the remaining lift comes from size, type and fit-out, not from knowing the sector better.

Where it is weakest

mean signed log error by price quintile · positive means the model asked too much

The error is not evenly spread. Sorted into price fifths, the mean signed log error runs +0.129 → +0.033 → −0.001 → −0.033 → −0.129, monotonically. The model overprices the cheapest homes and underprices the most expensive — the classic pull toward the middle of a log-target regression. The cheapest fifth is also the least accurate in percentage terms, at 27.6% MAPE against the overall 21.4%. Treat the estimate as weakest under about ₹1 crore and above about ₹10 crore.

Methodology

From raw listings to this page

Six stages, each one a command in the homecast CLI and each one covered by tests. The city is a parameter throughout, which is why three more are already wired into the selector above.

Collect

3,803 raw Gurgaon listings: price, area, bedrooms, bathrooms, furnishing, age, and a per-listing amenity score, keyed to a sector.

Clean

−126 exact duplicates, −17 listings with no price, −60 percentile outliers on ₹/sq.ft. and area. 3,600 rows survive, with the row count logged at every step.

Engineer

Eight features. The sector becomes its median ₹/sq.ft. — a target encoding, so it is re-learned inside each training fold rather than once up front.

Train

Gradient-boosted regression on log price. n_estimators 300 · max_depth 3 · learning_rate 0.06 · subsample 0.9 · seed 7. No tuning: the defaults already beat both baselines.

Evaluate

5-fold cross-validation against the two rules an agent would otherwise use — sector-median ₹/sq.ft. × area, and city-median ₹/sq.ft. × area. The 10th–90th percentile of the log errors becomes the band: −27.4% to +36.7% around the estimate. Near-symmetric in log space; the lopsided percentages are just what exponentiating a symmetric log interval does.

Ship

All 300 trees export to JSON as plain arrays. This page walks them in about ten lines of JavaScript; a test asserts that walker matches scikit-learn to 1e-9, so the page can never drift from the model.
